Charlottesville, VA

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Computer Engineering at the University of Virginia, advised by Prof. Mathews Jacob. My research sits at the intersection of generative modeling and statistical machine learning: I develop principled methods built on diffusion models, flow matching, and energy-based models, and use them to solve inverse problems, align representations across modalities, and make large language models reason more efficiently.

Before UVA, I received an M.S. in Statistics and Data Science from Northwestern University and a B.Sc. in Applied Mathematics with First Class Honours from the University of Liverpool. I also worked as a researcher at the University of Pittsburgh Medical Center (UPMC) on medical image segmentation and survival analysis, and as an undergraduate I applied machine learning to Gaia astrometric data, contributing to three papers in The Astrophysical Journal.

Research interests

  • Generative models: diffusion models, flow matching, energy-based models, optimal transport
  • Inverse problems: Bayesian posterior sampling with generative priors, computational MRI
  • Efficient LLM reasoning: model merging, long-to-short chain-of-thought compression
  • Multimodal learning: representation alignment across vision, language, and single-cell data
  • Medical image analysis: segmentation and outcome prediction

selected publications

  1. NeurIPS
    Low-Rank Hierarchical Merging for Efficient Long-to-Short Reasoning
    Z. Yu, X. Zhang, W. Zhao, and S. Wang
    In Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS), 2026
    Abs

    Low-Rank Hierarchical Merging (LHM) is a training-free, plug-and-play framework that combines the reasoning ability of long chain-of-thought models with the concise generation of their base LLMs through layer-adaptive parameter merging. Merging coefficients come from a Layer Second-Order Sensitivity (LSOS) index, built from task-data-free Hessian trace estimates and task-vector norms, together with a dual-constraint coefficient mapping that protects reasoning-critical layers. On the 1.5B Qwen setting, LHM reaches 48.8% average accuracy across six math benchmarks, 3.2 points above ORCA-AIM, while shortening the R1 model’s responses by 59.7%; the gains carry over to Llama and Mistral model pairs with no added inference latency.

  2. NeurIPS
    Flow-Guided Target-Space Alignment via Path Consistency
    R. Yuan*, Z. Yu*, W. Gao, W. Chen, X. Tang, and L. Tang
    In Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS), 2026
    Abs

    FlowAlign is a two-stage framework for fixed-target multimodal alignment. It first learns source-conditioned flow fields from paired source and target representations, then reuses the frozen field as a differentiable path-consistency supervisor for mapping new source samples. We derive endpoint-error bounds that tie mapping accuracy to the consistency loss and the field residual. FlowAlign outperforms baselines such as Seurat v5, GLUE, and GENOT on single-cell multi-omics (PBMC/BMMC) and ranks first on DINOv3-to-SigLIP2 image-text alignment (CUBICC).

  3. Under Review
    CLIMB-Flow: Coupled Linear Inverse Posterior Sampling via Multiscale-Based Flow
    Z. Yu, R. Yuan, J. R. Chand, S. Murtaza, and M. Jacob
    2026
    Abs

    CLIMB-Flow is a training-free stochastic solver for linear inverse problems with cascaded pixel-space flow models. It samples an exact stage-wise Bayesian posterior over coupled clean and noise endpoints using split Gibbs-Langevin dynamics with a measurement-aware conjugate-gradient preconditioner, and proceeds coarse-to-fine while keeping the original linear observation model. Experiments cover inpainting, deblurring, 4x super-resolution, and 8x accelerated MRI on ImageNet-256, AFHQ-512, and fastMRI.

  4. SPIE
    3D spatial and channel reconstruction for large image patch based segmentation of knee anatomical structures on MRI
    Z. Yu, X. Zhao, J. Singh, J. Wang, T. Yu, D. Barot, and J. Pu
    In Medical Imaging 2024: Image Processing, Proc. SPIE 12926, 2024
    Oral Abs DOI

    Oral presentation at SPIE Medical Imaging 2024.

    We introduce a 3D spatial-and-channel reconstruction module that suppresses redundant features in nnU-Net, enabling larger volumetric input patches for segmenting knee anatomical structures on MRI. On 183 radiologist-annotated knee MRI scans covering nine structures, it raises mean Dice from 0.811 to 0.823 at the same patch size with fewer parameters, and reaches 0.825 with larger patches.
